Why micro water bodies

The small waters that make the large ones

Big dams, reservoirs and rivers draw the attention of governments and scholars. The channels, canals, weirs, streams, ponds and tanks that feed them rarely do — yet they gather water across hills and plains, sustain everyday life, and bring water, culture and people together.

We read each of these places through thick description: what history records, what rituals still practise, what writers have imagined, and what ecology now warns. The result is a map you can travel in depth, not only across.

Water and memory are utterly intertwined.Owain Jones & Heather Green, “Rivers—Mouths—Tides—Memories” (2021)
Four layers of memory

One water body, four ways of remembering

Every place on the map opens into four layers, each told by its own fish: an aged mahseer for history, a rohu dressed for a wedding for culture, a scholarly hilsa for literature and a distressed magur for the environment.
